# Domain adapter: business and operations

Applies when the deliverable is a business decision or artifact: plans,
budgets, pricing, proposals, pitch decks, vendor choices, process docs, or
emails that commit the business. The loop is unchanged; these definitions
replace coding defaults.

## Minimum evidence set (binding, before any recommendation)

1. **The real numbers**: current costs, prices, dates, and business records,
   never memory or “typical” figures.
2. **The binding constraint**: budget, deadline, headcount, or regulation.
3. **Who is affected**: customers, partners, staff, or cash flow named before
   recommending.

## Evidence and primary sources

The business's own documents (PRD, business plan, prior decisions, contracts)
> current external sources (vendor pricing, regulations, market rates) >
general knowledge. Prior owner decisions are settled; do not re-litigate them.

## Authority order

Explicit owner/user decisions > written strategy and brand documents > the
deliverable brief > industry convention. Surface conflicts between brief and
strategy; never silently resolve them.

## Verification by observation

- Recompute and show arithmetic; totals must reconcile with constraints.
- Every external commitment traces to a current source opened in the task.
- Outward-facing actions (sent, published, signed, purchased) are irreversible
  and require user confirmation under the method's authorization gate.

## Fraud table (for fable-judge)

| Fraud | Symptom |
|---|---|
| Budget fiction | Line items exceed the stated budget without saying so |
| Hockey-stick projections | Growth numbers have no mechanism or basis |
| Invented market figures | TAM or benchmark numbers have no real source |
| Silent scope changes | Deliverable drifts from the brief without flagging it |
| Stale commitments | Prices, terms, or regulations come from memory |
| Decision re-litigation | Settled owner choices are reopened |

## Done, by example

“The budget plan is done” means every line item is current and reconciled,
trade-offs are named, and open decisions are listed. Not: “a reasonable-looking
allocation.”
