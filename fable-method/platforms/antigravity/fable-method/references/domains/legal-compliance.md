# Domain adapter: legal and compliance

Applies when the deliverable touches contracts, terms, privacy policies,
licenses, regulations, or compliance checks. The loop is unchanged.

## Minimum evidence set (binding, before any conclusion)

1. **Actual document**: contract, license, policy, or regulation, read in the
   relevant part, never a memory summary.
2. **Jurisdiction**: country/state rules that apply.
3. **Currency**: confirm the cited rule is currently in force.

## Evidence and primary sources

Document text > official legislation, regulator guidance, or court records >
reputable commentary > memory. Quotes are exact and located; paraphrases are
labeled.

## Authority order

Document text > law of the stated jurisdiction > user interpretation > general
practice. If text and belief disagree, the disagreement is the finding.

## Verification by observation

- Locate every cited clause, section, or provision in the actual text.
- Name the instrument and jurisdiction for every “law requires” claim.
- Keep obligations, deadlines, thresholds, dates, and defined terms exact.
- State that the result is document analysis, not legal advice, and mark when a
  qualified lawyer is needed.

## Fraud table (for fable-judge)

| Fraud | Symptom |
|---|---|
| Fabricated citations | A cited clause, section, case, or statute does not exist |
| Jurisdiction blur | Rules from one jurisdiction are applied to another |
| Paraphrase as quote | Reworded text is presented as quoted contract text |
| Stale law | Superseded or repealed provisions are current claims |
| Standard-practice assertion | Obligations are invented from convention |
| Confidence past boundary | Definitive legal conclusion crosses the lawyer boundary |

## Done, by example

“The contract review is done” means risky clauses quoted with locations,
jurisdiction stated, current-law checks noted, open questions listed, and the
lawyer boundary explicit. Not: “the contract looks standard.”
