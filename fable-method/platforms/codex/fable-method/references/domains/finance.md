# Domain adapter: finance

Applies when the deliverable involves money decisions or figures: cost
comparisons, pricing, budgets, tax/interest, investment or loan comparisons,
payback, ROI, or accounting-adjacent work. The loop is unchanged.

## Minimum evidence set (binding, before any figure)

1. **Current rates and prices**, fetched now: rates, fees, exchange rates, tax
   thresholds, and product prices.
2. **All-in cost**: fees, taxes, penalties, and exit costs, not only headline
   rate.
3. **Actual situation**: amounts, timeline, jurisdiction, and risk constraints.

## Evidence and primary sources

Official rate pages, regulators, institutional terms, and user figures are
primary. Aggregators are leads to verify, not sources to cite.

## Authority order

User constraints and jurisdiction > official/regulatory sources > institutional
terms > memory. State assumptions beside every projection.

## Verification by observation

- Every rate, fee, threshold, and price has an opened source and effective date.
- Recompute arithmetic with correct bases, periods, currencies, and totals.
- Compare like-for-like periods and nominal/real or gross/net bases.
- State that analysis is not regulated financial advice when a licensed
  professional boundary is near.

## Fraud table (for fable-judge)

| Fraud | Symptom |
|---|---|
| Stale rates | Old rates, tax bands, or prices are presented as current |
| Headline-rate comparison | Fees, taxes, or exit costs are omitted |
| Guarantee language | “Guaranteed” or “risk-free” has no basis |
| Base-rate abuse | Percentages use the wrong base or mismatched compounding |
| Cherry-picked windows | A flattering timeframe is chosen silently |
| Projection as fact | Forecasts omit assumptions |

## Done, by example

“The loan comparison is done” means current dated rates, all-in cost over the
actual term, shown arithmetic, assumptions, and the license boundary. Not:
“option A is cheaper.”
